We trained three models on the full dataset — all 47 engineered features, including issue_year and addr_state.

CHAPTER 03

The Discovery

AUC = 0.7363 looked great. SHAP told a different story.

The third most important feature had nothing to do with the borrower

We ran SHAP on v3 to understand what the model had learned. The result was striking.

CHAPTER 04

The Decision

Removing leakage cost us 1% AUC. It was the right call.

→
v4 (clean)
0.7286

Δ = −0.0077 AUC. Roughly 1% of performance.

Three reasons the trade was worth it

01

Production-readiness

A model dependent on calendar year cannot generalize to future data. If deployed in 2019, it would fail on 2020's COVID-driven defaults.

02

Fairness

Geography should not drive credit decisions. ZIP code and state have well-documented disparate-impact implications in lending.

03

Scientific integrity

Keeping leakage would mean reporting a number we knew was inflated. That is not engineering — that is self-deception.

CHAPTER 05

The Ablation Study

When the model hit the ceiling, we asked a simple question: why?

v4 stopped improving at 4,000 rounds — in every single fold. Early stopping never triggered. Either the learning rate was too high, or we needed more rounds. We designed a controlled experiment to find out.

Version learning_rate max_rounds AUC avg_best_iter
v4 0.011 4,000 0.7286 3,989 ⚠️
v5 ✅ 0.011 8,000 0.7287 4,034
v6 0.025 8,000 0.7283 1,855

The finding

With a higher ceiling, v5 finally triggered early stopping at 4,034 rounds. The bottleneck was not the learning rate — it was the total training budget.

But more importantly: v5 and v6 landed at the same AUC, despite v6 using 50% fewer trees. The dataset had a ceiling. We had reached it.

CHAPTER 06

The Golden Finding

Freed from leakage, the model found the right signal.

After removing leakage features, we re-ran SHAP on v5. Something remarkable had happened.

Feature v3 Rank v5 Rank Change
sub_grade 1 1 —
term 2 2 —
issue_year 3 removed 🗑️
grade 4 3 ↑ 1
dti 5 4 ↑ 1
home_ownership 6 5 ↑ 1
addr_state 7 removed 🗑️
int_rate 14 6 ↑ 8

Why this matters

In v3, the model relied on issue_year and addr_state — proxy signals that happened to correlate with outcomes in the training data.

In v5, freed from those crutches, the model leaned on int_rate — the interest rate itself. And the interest rate is a risk signal. Banks set it based on their own assessment of default probability.

The model had stopped cheating and started learning.

In Summary

What we learned along the way.

01

Best model ≠ highest AUC

A model that is production-ready, fair, and calibrated is worth more than one with 1% higher AUC and hidden leakage.

02

SHAP is not optional

AUC alone would never have revealed the leakage. Interpretation is what separates engineering from guessing.

03

When a model hits a ceiling, check the data first

The Ablation Study proved the bottleneck was not hyperparameters, it was the signal in the data itself.

04

Calibration is what makes a model useful

AUC = 0.73 with perfect calibration is worth more to a bank than AUC = 0.80 with a miscalibrated score.

05

Deployment is half the work

The other half is documentation, interpretation, and honest reporting. A model that cannot be explained is a model that cannot be trusted.
